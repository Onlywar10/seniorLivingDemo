Reviewed 1 Aug 2026
Visiting hours

Any day, 8 am to 8 pm, no appointment needed. After 8 pm ring the bell; the night nurse will let you in. Overnight stays in your family member's apartment are fine for up to three nights, just tell the front desk. During an outbreak the alert at the top of this page takes precedence.

Pets

Cats and dogs under 25 lb may live with residents who can care for them, with current vaccinations and a $500 refundable deposit. Visiting dogs are welcome on a lead in the courtyard and first-floor common areas, not in dining rooms. Two resident cats, Biscuit and Pearl, live on the first floor.

Transport

The bus does medical appointments within 15 miles on Tuesdays and Thursdays from 8:30 am, and the shopping run on Wednesdays at 10 am. Book with Roy (ext. 160) by noon the day before. Wheelchair lift on board. Outside those hours we can arrange a paid escort at $35 an hour.

Guest meals

Join your family member for any meal: $9, or $12 for the Sunday roast, added to the monthly invoice. Tell the front desk by 10 am so the kitchen sets a place. Private dining room for up to 12 is free to reserve for birthdays; give Luis a week's notice.

Mail and packages

Address mail to the resident's name and apartment number at 1400 Fern Street. Letters are delivered to apartments by 2 pm. Packages are signed for at the front desk and logged; we call the resident, and we can open and put things away if that has been agreed in the care plan.

Built to be read at 78, or on a phone in a hospital car park

The person reading this page is often the resident herself, or a daughter making a decision under stress on a small screen. So this site is built to a standard, not a style.

The publishing platform behind this page was built for school districts, which are legally required to meet WCAG 2.1 AA under the 2024 update to Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act. Every template, table and document check on this page comes from that obligation.

  • Text is 17 px or larger everywhere, 18 px for body copy, and the page reflows at 200% zoom without side-scrolling.
  • Contrast above WCAG AA on every colour pairing: nothing below 4.5:1, body text above 10:1.
  • Buttons and links are at least 44 px tall so they can be hit with a thumb or a shaky hand.
  • Visible focus outline on every control for people who use a keyboard.
  • Real headings and real tables with header cells, so a screen reader can read the rate table row by row.
  • Nothing is conveyed by colour alone. The calendar categories, the citation row and the availability dots all carry text.
  • Documents are checked before they publish. A PDF without tags or a scanned image is blocked until it is fixed.

About this demo

Larkmoor House is fictional. Every name, number, price and date on this page was written for this demonstration; the phone numbers are 555 numbers. The structure is the point.

Almost every senior living website is a brochure: a hero photograph, a promise of a "robust activity calendar", and a lead form. The real information, what happens on a Tuesday, who works there, what it costs, what the state found, is either in a PDF, on a resident-built site on another domain, or not published at all.

This page argues that in senior living the information is the sales asset. A real, dated calendar is un-fakeable: a community with thin programming cannot publish one. A stock photo is a promise; this week's schedule is evidence. So proof comes before pitch, and there is no hero image and no lead form.

The publishing model is borrowed from school district websites, which already publish calendars, menus, staff directories, documents and alerts continuously to a community that belongs to them. It is adapted here as six content modules, each with a different owner inside the building and its own publishing lane. That ownership split is why this needs a governed platform, not a website builder.

What to look for

  1. Press "Show module structure" in the header. Every section is labelled with its module, its editor, the content type underneath and whether it publishes immediately or needs approval.
  2. Switch the audience near the top. Only one sentence and one button change; the content is one set, framed three ways: the adult child shopping after a crisis, the family member checking on this week, and the remote sibling who has never visited.
  3. Read the calendar. Real times, named rooms, a moved event tied to the live notice. This is the module that cannot be faked.
  4. Find the 2023 citation in the inspection record, corrected in 14 days. An all-clean history reads as marketing; a small problem fixed quickly is what makes the clean years credible.
  5. Notice what is missing: no hero, no stock photography, no lead form. The photos are honest placeholders with real captions and dates.
